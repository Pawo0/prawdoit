# 04 — Methodology: SPRAWDŹ, fact-checking standards, FIMI frameworks, verdict rules

Research date: 2026-11-21. Scope: what methodology Prawdowid should follow and say it follows, so that an MSZ/NASK jury sees it as compatible with IFCN/EFCSN/EDMO/NASK practice. The challenge text (`research/opis_wyzwania_prawdowid.pdf`) does **not** mention "SPRAWDŹ" anywhere. It points to "praktyki NASK dotyczące analizy dezinformacji" and "materiały NASK dotyczące dezinformacji", and NASK is where SPRAWDŹ comes from.

---

## 0. TL;DR

- **SPRAWDŹ is real and comes from NASK, not MSZ.** It is the 7-step citizen checklist from the NASK campaign **„Sprawdź, zanim udostępnisz”**. NASK coordinated it with the Ministerstwo Cyfryzacji and the UK Government Communications Service International, and it launched in November 2023. The letters are **S**ensacyjność, **P**rowokowanie, **R**acjonalność, **A**utor, **W**adliwy kontekst, **D**yskredytacja, **Ź**ródła.
- **NASK's current version (2025) is SPRAWDZAM**, a 9-letter mnemonic: Sensacja, Prowokacja, Racjonalność, Autor, Wybiórczość, Dyskredytacja, Zamiar, Analiza, Manipulacja. It is on nask.pl/dezinfo and is reused on gov.pl by the Ministerstwo Klimatu i Środowiska.
- The original campaign domain `sprawdzam.info` **has lapsed**. It now hosts an unrelated business site, so **do not link it**.
- No MSZ-specific "SPRAWDŹ" was found. MSZ's own material (Departament Komunikacji Strategicznej i Przeciwdziałania Dezinformacji Międzynarodowej, Rada Konsultacyjna ds. Odporności na Dezinformację Międzynarodową) gives no citizen checklist. Its recommendations stress prebunking, fact-checker support and fast public information.
- **Recommendation:** use SPRAWDŹ/SPRAWDZAM as an *educational/prebunking layer* ("sygnały ostrzegawcze"), not as an input to the verdict. The verdict has to come from evidence-based rules (§7). The challenge forbids judging content by domain, author, channel or popularity, and rhetoric signals are a close cousin of that.

## 1. SPRAWDŹ: what it is, exactly

### 1.1 SPRAWDŹ (7 steps): campaign „Sprawdź, zanim udostępnisz” (NASK, Nov 2023)

| Letter | Polish term | What the citizen checks |
|---|---|---|
| **S** | **Sensacyjność** | Are the emotions the message provokes justified? Sensational headlines mislead. |
| **P** | **Prowokowanie** | Is the message only trying to start a quarrel or undermine trust in reliable sources? |
| **R** | **Racjonalność** | Is the content logical? Does it separate facts from opinions? |
| **A** | **Autor** | Who wrote it? An unnamed or unverifiable author warrants more doubt. |
| **W** | **Wadliwy kontekst** | Is the context presented fairly? Watch for selective quotes and cropped or manipulated graphics. |
| **D** | **Dyskredytacja** | Does it aim to discredit a person or group, or to deepen social divisions? |
| **Ź** | **Źródła** | Does the cited research or data exist, and is it reported accurately? |

Sources, two independent secondary write-ups that agree on all 7 letters:
- Antyweb, „Uważaj na fake newsy” (≈24.11.2023): https://antyweb.pl/uwazaj-na-fake-newsy
- Warto Wiedzieć, „Kampania Sprawdź, zanim udostępnisz” (30.11.2023): https://wartowiedziec.pl/serwis-glowny/69-styl-zycia/70834-kampania-sprawdz-zanim-udostepnisz
- NASK archive news item confirming the 7-step method, its partners (MC + UK GCSI, NASK coordinating) and the campaign site sprawdzam.info. The item does not list the letters: https://archiwum.nask.pl/pl/aktualnosci/5301,Sprawdz-zanim-udostepnisz.html
- Caveat: I could not retrieve the original campaign page. `sprawdzam.info` now serves an unrelated WordPress site ("Sprawdź kontrahenta"), and web.archive.org was not reachable from this environment.

### 1.2 SPRAWDZAM (9 letters): current NASK version (nask.pl/dezinfo, magazyn 22.04.2025)

| Letter | Term | Check |
|---|---|---|
| S | SENSACJA | Is the emotion (fear, outrage) justified? Calm down before sharing. |
| P | PROWOKACJA | Does it aim to stir controversy? Keep the discussion factual. |
| R | RACJONALNOŚĆ | Are the claims consistent and the conclusions justified? |
| A | AUTOR | Who is the author, and is the organisation trustworthy? Whose interest does sharing serve? |
| W | WYBIÓRCZOŚĆ | Is context missing? Look for quotes out of context, clipped videos, selective data. |
| D | DYSKREDYTACJA | Does it blame or defame people or groups? Report offensive content. |
| Z | ZAMIAR | Intent defines disinformation. Who is harmed, and who benefits? |
| A | ANALIZA | Pause and assess calmly. Verify with reliable sources. |
| M | MANIPULACJA | Are subjective feelings presented as facts? Are context and data distorted? Separate opinion from fact. |

- NASK magazyn, „SPRAWDZAM – jak się chronić przed dezinformacją?” (22.04.2025): https://www.nask.pl/magazyn/sprawdzam-jak-sie-chronic-przed-dezinformacja
- NASK campaign hub „Chroń się przed dezinformacją” (OAD NASK + Ministerstwo Cyfryzacji): https://www.nask.pl/dezinfo
- Reuse by Ministerstwo Klimatu i Środowiska (cites NASK): https://www.gov.pl/web/klimat/dezinfo-sprawdzam

### 1.3 Other official Polish checklists (closest relatives)

- **NASK „Era (dez)informacji” (2024, CC BY-NC 4.0, pp. 24–27), 5 steps of „Samodzielna weryfikacja informacji”.** Steps: 1) Sprawdź wiarygodność źródła; 2) Oceń rzetelność i logikę przedstawionej sytuacji; 3) Zweryfikuj, czy tę samą informację potwierdza inne źródło (official gov.pl communications, fact-checking NGOs, academia); 4) Odróżnij opinię autora od faktów; 5) Zgłoś podejrzenie o rozpowszechnianiu dezinformacji. The guide also has a technique taxonomy: podszywanie się, treści wprowadzające w błąd, fałszywy kontekst, cheapfake, deepfake, clickbait. https://www.nask.pl/media/2025/03/Era-Dezinfo_publikacja.pdf
- **gov.pl Baza wiedzy, „Rozpoznawanie nieprawdziwych informacji”, 5 steps.** Analyse the site, verify the cited sources, check structure and tone (facts vs opinions, does the headline match), compare with another reliable source, check how the people involved are portrayed. https://www.gov.pl/web/baza-wiedzy/rozpoznawanie-nieprawdziwych-informacji
- **NASK „Kodeks Dobrych Praktyk. Dezinformacja” (2022).** Co-written by 11 organisations incl. Demagog. Gives definitions of **informacja / opinia / spekulacja / plotka**: a plotka is so vague about time, place and people that it is "całkowicie nieweryfikowalna". Its communication rule is directly usable: *when reporting on disinformation, use screenshots rather than active links, or mark links `rel="nofollow"`, so you don't amplify the narrative.* https://zut.edu.pl/fileadmin/pliki/SSOiKN/NASK-Dezinformacja_kodeks_dobrych_praktyk.pdf ; summary: https://www.gov.pl/web/baza-wiedzy/dezinformacja-pomoze-kodeks-dobrych-praktyk
- **International:** **SIFT** (Mike Caulfield: Stop, Investigate the source, Find better coverage, Trace claims to the original context) and **lateral reading** (Wineburg & McGrew 2019, Stanford History Education Group / Civic Online Reasoning). SPRAWDŹ's Ź and W map onto SIFT's "F" and "T".

### 1.4 How to embed SPRAWDŹ in Prawdowid without breaking the rules

| SPRAWDŹ step | What Prawdowid shows | Does it affect the verdict? |
|---|---|---|
| S Sensacyjność | "Język silnie emocjonalny" signal, with quoted words | **No.** Educational only. |
| P Prowokowanie | "Treść nakłania do sporu / podważa zaufanie" signal | **No** |
| R Racjonalność | Claim decomposition: which parts are facts and which are opinions or predictions | Indirectly. Opinions become *nie podlega weryfikacji*. |
| A Autor | "Kto to opublikował?" box: publisher/author as found, plus a neutral source-status note (e.g. EU sanctions) | **No.** The challenge forbids it. |
| W Wadliwy kontekst | Date/place mismatch, earliest known appearance of an image, superseded data | **Yes, via evidence.** Drives *wprowadzające w błąd* when sourced. |
| D Dyskredytacja | "Treść wymierzona w grupę/osobę" signal + link to report to NASK or the platform | **No** |
| Ź Źródła | Do the cited sources exist, and do they say what is claimed? (quote check) | **Yes**. This is the core evidence step. |

UI wording suggestion: a collapsible panel „Sprawdź sam — metoda SPRAWDŹ (NASK)”, with each letter ticked or flagged and a link to nask.pl/dezinfo. This is prebunking-style technique education, which has good evidence behind it (§6.6). It also shows the jury that Prawdowid speaks NASK's language.

## 2. Standards that should shape product features

### 2.1 IFCN Code of Principles: 5 commitments
Source: https://ifcncodeofprinciples.poynter.org/the-commitments

| Commitment | Key requirement | Prawdowid feature |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Non-partisanship & fairness | "Same high standards of evidence and judgment for equivalent claims"; don't focus unduly on one side; publish how claims are selected | Deterministic rules (§7) applied identically. A **symmetry test** in eval (mirrored claims about different parties). Non-partisanship statement on the Metodologia page. |
| 2. Standards & transparency of sources | Identify the source of all significant evidence, with links, "so that users can replicate their work"; best available primary sources; >1 named source for key elements | Evidence card: title, publisher, published date, retrieved date, link, verbatim fragment. Tier labels. ≥2 independent groups, except for self-authoritative primary sources. |
| 3. Transparency of funding & organisation | Funding sources, ownership, who exercises editorial control, contact | "O projekcie" page: team, hackathon context, model/provider, hosting location, no paid placement. |
| 4. Standards & transparency of methodology | Publish the methodology for selecting, researching, writing and publishing; show evidence for and against; invite submissions; say what can and cannot be checked | Metodologia page (§9). Show refuting *and* supporting evidence. The "nie podlega weryfikacji" category is explained. |
| 5. Open & honest corrections | Visible corrections policy; correct openly so people who saw the original see the fix | „Zgłoś błąd” on every result. Public **rejestr korekt**. Results versioned with "stan wiedzy na dzień". |

### 2.2 EFCSN European Code of Standards (Articles 2–4 are the relevant ones)
Source: https://efcsn.com/code-of-standards/

- **Art. 2 Methodology:** publish how investigations are selected, researched and completed, with a *definition of any rating system*. Focus on public interest. Run a public submission channel and **state what is and isn't fact-checkable**. Give evidence for every pertinent factual statement, *including evidence that undermines the claim*. Use **at least two sources for the central claim where possible, preferring primary sources**. Write in **"precise, factual and non-emotive language"** without overstating conclusions. Have someone other than the author edit (analogue for us: a second verifier pass or human review). Archive claims and evidence (best practice). Annex 12 sets minimum criteria for narrative analysis.
- **Art. 3 Ethics:** non-partisanship (no party endorsements, conflict-of-interest disclosure). Privacy and safety: anonymise personal data that could enable harassment, protect minors. **Honest corrections:** a correction note in the same publication, update notes for new information, and for substantial errors an explanation of how the error happened. Publish the corrections policy and a record of corrections.
- **Art. 4 Transparency:** organisational and financial transparency.
- No AI-specific clauses. Our AI disclosure therefore comes from the AI Act (§8).

### 2.3 EDMO
- **Guiding Principles for the EDMO Network:** Independence, Integrity, Transparency, Neutrality ("strict policy of neutrality"), Pluralism, Diversity & inclusion, Scientifically grounded interventions. https://edmohub.ie/index.php/guiding-principles/ (PDF copy: https://becid.eu/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Guiding-Principles-for-the-EDMO-Network.pdf)
- **Fact-checking repository:** EDMO aggregates fact-checking articles from its hubs and network (>40 organisations, 15 hubs as of 2025). Poland is covered by **CEDMO** (CZ/SK/PL), of which Demagog is a member. Use it as a type-B corpus (§7). https://edmo.eu/ , https://cedmohub.eu/
- Product translation: neutral wording; pluralism of the source catalogue (several independent publishers per topic); everything published and auditable.

## 3. NASK practice (what an NASK juror will recognise)

- **Unit:** Ośrodek Analizy Dezinformacji (OAD) NASK-PIB, which includes the Zespół Szybkiego Reagowania i Wykrywania Dezinformacji. It monitors social media 7 days a week and handles citizen reports. https://oad.nask.pl/en/about-us
- **Terminology** (from Wardle & Derakhshan, Council of Europe 2017): **misinformacja** is false content shared without intent to harm. **Dezinformacja** is false or misleading content deliberately created to harm or to gain something. **Malinformacja** is true content used maliciously, e.g. doxxing. NASK stresses that **intent** defines disinformation, and Prawdowid cannot know intent. **So Prawdowid should say "nieprawdziwe"/"wprowadzające w błąd", never "dezinformacja", about a user-submitted item.** "Dezinformacja" belongs in analyst/FIMI views after human assessment.
- **Technique tags NASK uses** (nask.pl/dezinfo, Era (dez)informacji): clickbait, język emocjonalny, podszywanie się, cherry picking, fałszywa przyczyna, dowód anegdotyczny, fałszywy kontekst, treści wprowadzające w błąd, cheapfake, deepfake. Reuse these exact Polish labels as "rozpoznana technika" tags.
- **How NASK classifies reports** (report „Reakcja platform społecznościowych na zgłoszenia w 2025 roku”, NASK 2026, CC BY-NC 4.0, ch. „Metodologia obsługi zgłoszeń”). Internal categories: **fałszywa informacja, manipulacja, podszywanie się** (+ generic treść). A report is "zasadne" only if it plausibly breaches platform terms or law. Outcomes are tracked as odrzucone / poddane moderacji / usunięte. NASK reported >46k items to platforms in 2025, and 12% were removed. https://nask.pl/media/2026/06/Reakcja-platform-spolecznosciowych-na-zgloszenia.pdf
- **Reporting channels Prawdowid should route users to (signposting, not auto-forwarding):**
  - Disinformation: **https://www.zglos-dezinformacje.nask.pl**, e-mail **dezinformacja@nask.pl**
  - Phishing / scam SMS: forward to **8080** (CERT Polska); incidents at **https://incydent.cert.pl**
  - Illegal content such as CSAM: **Dyżurnet.pl** (NASK). Note: Dyżurnet is *not* a disinformation channel.
  - Platform notice (DSA notice-and-action), via the platform's own report form.
- **Communication rule** (Kodeks Dobrych Praktyk): don't amplify. Show screenshots/archived snippets of the disinformation itself rather than live links, or use `rel="nofollow ugc"`.
- **Government campaigns** for context: MC + NASK „Nie bądź internetowym trollem” (2025), relaunch 17.10–16.11.2025 on national TV: https://www.gov.pl/web/cyfryzacja/bezpieczenstwo-informacyjne-to-wspolna-sprawa-ministerstwo-cyfryzacji-wznawia-ogolnopolska-kampanie-spoleczna

- What this implies: MSZ values prebunking, speed, cooperation with fact-checkers, and domestic/EEA tech. That favours a self-hostable stack with EEA processing.

### 4.2 EEAS FIMI vocabulary (4th Threat Report, March 2026, "Dismantling the FIMI House of Cards")
- **FIMI** is "a mostly non-illegal pattern of behaviour that threatens or has the potential to negatively impact values, procedures and political processes… manipulative… intentional and coordinated". Note: *behaviour*, not *truth value*. A true statement can be part of FIMI, and a false one need not be.
- **Incident** (observables + TTPs, intent to deceive) → **Campaign** → **IMS (Information Manipulation Set)** → technical attribution to a **Threat Actor**. All 540 incidents of 2025 were **encoded in STIX**. 5D radar (Dismiss/Distort/Distract/Dismay/Divide), with references to DISARM techniques T0075 Dismiss, T0076 Distort and T0079 Divide.
- Report: https://www.eeas.europa.eu/eeas/4th-eeas-annual-report-foreign-information-manipulation-and-interference-threats_en (PDF: https://www.eeas.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026/documents/EEAS%204th%20Threat%20Report_web.pdf). Earlier reports: 2nd, "A Framework for Networked Defence" (2024), https://www.eeas.europa.eu/eeas/2nd-eeas-report-foreign-information-manipulation-and-interference-threats_en ; 3rd, "Exposing the architecture of FIMI operations" (2025, FIMI Exposure Matrix of official / state-controlled / state-linked / state-aligned channels), https://www.eeas.europa.eu/eeas/3rd-eeas-report-foreign-information-manipulation-and-interference-threats_en
- **ABCDE** (Pamment 2020, Carnegie) is the analytical frame: **A**ctor, **B**ehaviour, **C**ontent, **D**egree (reach/scale), **E**ffect. Prawdowid only judges **C** (content veracity) for citizens. B/D/E (e.g. many similar reports, cross-channel spread) can be shown *only in the analyst dashboard*. **A** (attribution) must never be inferred automatically.
- **DISARM** (https://www.disarm.foundation/, https://github.com/DISARMFoundation/DISARMframeworks) has a **Red framework** (incident creator TTPs, phases Plan → Prepare → Execute → Assess) and a **Blue framework** (countermeasures). STIX 2.1 representation: techniques are `attack-pattern`, tactics `x-mitre-tactic`, matrix `x-mitre-matrix` (https://github.com/DISARMFoundation/DISARM-STIX2). OpenCTI has a DISARM connector that imports TTPs, kill-chain phases and courses of action (https://hub.filigran.io/cybersecurity-solutions/opencti-integrations/disarm-framework), plus disinformation-specific entities such as Narrative (https://filigran.io/how-opencti-helps-to-fight-disinformation-and-foreign-interferences/).

## 5. Demagog & AFP Sprawdzam methodology, and the mapping to Prawdowid classes

### 5.1 Demagog (IFCN since 05.2019; EFCSN; CEDMO; member of the MSZ Rada Odporności)
- **Statements of public figures, 5 ratings:** *prawda*, *częściowa prawda*, *fałsz*, *manipulacja*, *nieweryfikowalne*. History: originally 4 ratings; a 5th, „częściowy fałsz”, was added in 2022 and later renamed „częściowa prawda”, and some FAQ pages still use the old name. Definitions, paraphrased from methodology pages and snippets:
  - **Prawda:** confirmed by **two reliable and independent sources** (or one, if it is the only adequate source); primary sources preferred.
  - **Częściowa prawda:** true and false elements mixed, but the false part does not distort the thesis.
  - **Fałsz:** not consistent with any publicly available representative and reliable source. Can include outdated data contradicted by newer data. "Fałsz nie jest równoznaczny z kłamstwem": Demagog does not judge intent.
  - **Manipulacja:** misleads by bending facts: omitting important context, cherry-picking, comparing non-comparable data, exaggerating achievements. Demagog stresses it is *not* a milder rating than fałsz.
  - **Nieweryfikowalne:** **cannot be checked in any available source**. This is an *evidence-absence* category, not an *opinion* category (important for mapping, see below).
- **Fake news (Meta 3PFC labels used by Demagog):** fałsz, przeróbka (altered media), częściowy fałsz, brak kontekstu, satyra, prawda (Demagog FAQ).
- **Selection:** public relevance, balanced across parties for statements. For fake news: reach, virality, potential harm, reader submissions.
- URLs: https://demagog.org.pl/metodologia/ , https://demagog.org.pl/faq-demagoga/ , https://demagog.org.pl/jak-sprawdzamy-fake-newsy/ , methodology history: https://demagog.org.pl/analizy_i_raporty/jak-zmieniala-sie-metodologia-i-artykuly-w-demagogu-od-krotkich-wyjasnien-do-poglebionych-analiz/ (demagog.org.pl returns 403 to bots, so wording is from search snippets; verify exact text by hand).

### 5.2 AFP Sprawdzam (AFP Fact Check, Polish service, Warsaw; IFCN + EFCSN verified)
- **Selection:** "dubious claims circulating online which are **viral, impactful and potentially harmful**", with priority for health/life, democratic processes and hate speech. "**AFP fact-checking teams verify facts, not opinions or beliefs.**"
- **Evidence bar:** "If we are unable to establish strong and cross-checked evidence, **we will not publish** a fact-check." At least **two independent sources** for the central claim. Primary sources preferred. Anonymous sources only exceptionally. Steps are shown transparently (links, screenshots, archives). The claimant is contacted where relevant.
- **Corrections:** user error reports; edit vs. (rarely) removal; update notes; a dated corrections list.
- **Ratings:** AFP's international vocabulary, aligned with Meta 3PFC: *False, Altered, Partly false, Missing context, Satire, True*. AFP articles also use "Misleading". I could not fetch AFP's own definitions (factcheck.afp.com blocks bots), so verify by hand.
- URLs: https://factcheck.afp.com/How-we-work (copy: https://admohub.eu/en/fact-checking/afp-how-we-work/), EFCSN application: https://members.efcsn.com/application/applicationafp-agence-france-presse-5d10 , https://sprawdzam.afp.com , Meta ratings: https://transparency.meta.com/features/how-fact-checking-works/

**Consequence for corpus use:** AFP/Demagog fact-check corpora are heavily skewed towards false/misleading items, because they publish only what is debunkable and viral. *The absence of a fact-check is not evidence of truth.*

### 5.3 Mapping external labels to the 5 Prawdowid classes

An imported label counts as **one type-B evidence item**, and only after a **claim-match check** (same claim, same entity/time/place; not just topical similarity). A non-matching but similar fact-check is shown as „Podobne twierdzenie było już sprawdzane” (context, no verdict). This mirrors challenge scenario I.

| External label | Prawdowid class | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Demagog *prawda* / Meta-AFP *True* / FEVER *SUPPORTS* / AVeriTeC *Supported* | **POTWIERDZONE** | Check the fact-check's date against the claim's time scope. |
| Demagog *fałsz* / *False* / *REFUTES* / *Refuted* | **FAŁSZYWE** | |
| *przeróbka* / *Altered* (manipulated image/video/audio) | **FAŁSZYWE** + tag „materiał zmanipulowany” | Use WPROWADZAJĄCE if only cosmetic edits plus a misleading caption. |
| Demagog *manipulacja* / *Misleading* / *Missing context* / *brak kontekstu* | **WPROWADZAJĄCE W BŁĄD** | Name the sub-type: kontekst / wybiórczość / nieaktualne / błędny wniosek. |
| *częściowy fałsz* / *Partly false* | **WPROWADZAJĄCE W BŁĄD** | Better: decompose into atomic claims and rate each. |
| Demagog *częściowa prawda* | **Decompose**. If atomic split is impossible: **POTWIERDZONE** with "zastrzeżenia" note when the false part is peripheral, **WPROWADZAJĄCE W BŁĄD** when it changes the message | The Demagog definition says the false part "nie wypacza tezy". Don't automatically make it harsher than Demagog did. |
| Demagog *nieweryfikowalne* / AVeriTeC *Not Enough Evidence* / FEVER *NEI* / "Unproven" | **BRAK WYSTARCZAJĄCYCH DOWODÓW** | **Not** "nie podlega weryfikacji". Demagog's label means no sources are available, not that the item is an opinion. |
| AVeriTeC *Conflicting evidence / Cherrypicking* | Conflict → **BRAK WYSTARCZAJĄCYCH DOWODÓW** (flag „źródła sprzeczne”). Cherry-picking → **WPROWADZAJĄCE W BŁĄD** | Split by reason. |
| *Satire / satyra* | **NIE PODLEGA WERYFIKACJI** (tag „satyra”) | If satire is re-shared as real news, the re-shared claim is **WPROWADZAJĄCE W BŁĄD** or **FAŁSZYWE**. |
| Opinion / prediction / value judgment (not rated by AFP or Demagog) | **NIE PODLEGA WERYFIKACJI** | Extract any embedded factual premise as a separate claim. |
| EUvsDisinfo case ("disinformation" + disproof) | Context or type-B evidence for the **same** claim only | The outlet appearing in the EUvsDisinfo DB is **never** a verdict. |

## 6. Academic best practice (automated fact-checking + UX of corrections)

### 6.1 Pipeline (consensus architecture)
Guo, Schlichtkrull & Vlachos 2022, "A Survey on Automated Fact-Checking", TACL (https://doi.org/10.1162/tacl_a_00454): **claim detection (check-worthiness) → evidence retrieval → verdict prediction (stance/veracity) → justification production.** Add for real-world use:
1. **Claim normalisation**: rewrite a noisy post into a self-contained claim. CLEF CheckThat! 2025 Task 2 covered 20 languages **including Polish** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14828).
2. **Subjectivity / fact-vs-opinion detection** (CheckThat! 2023–2025 subjectivity tasks). This implements "odróżnianie faktów od opinii".
3. **Previously-fact-checked claim retrieval**: match against IFCN/EFCSN corpora before open retrieval (MultiClaim, Pikuliak et al. 2023, 206k fact-checks in ~40 languages; SemEval-2025 Task 7, https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10740. Check Polish coverage before relying on it).
4. **Question-decomposition evidence retrieval** (AVeriTeC style: generate sub-questions for who/what/when/where/how much, retrieve per question).
5. **Temporal reasoning**: CheckThat! 2026 Task 2 is about numerical *and temporal* claims (https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09516).

### 6.2 Benchmarks & label sets
- **FEVER** (Thorne et al. 2018, https://aclanthology.org/N18-1074/): SUPPORTS / REFUTES / NOT ENOUGH INFO. The "NEI" class institutionalises abstention.
- **AVeriTeC** (Schlichtkrull et al., NeurIPS 2023, https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13117): real-world claims with QA evidence. Labels: Supported / Refuted / **Not Enough Evidence** / **Conflicting Evidence/Cherrypicking**. The **AVeriTeC score counts a verdict as correct only if the retrieved evidence is adequate**. Adopt the same rule internally, since the jury's "poprawność cytowania ≥85%" metric is the same idea.
- AVeriTeC 2025 shared task (FEVER-8): open-weights ≤14B, 60 s/claim on a single 23 GB A10 GPU. The best score was only 0.33, so **real-world open retrieval is hard and abstention must be first-class.** https://aclanthology.org/2025.fever-1.22 (overview of 2024: https://aclanthology.org/2024.fever-1.1.pdf)

### 6.3 Abstention / selective prediction
- Selective classification: trade coverage for accuracy with a rejection option (Geifman & El-Yaniv 2017, https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08500). For LLMs, see the survey "Know Your Limits: A Survey of Abstention in LLMs" (Wen et al. 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.18418) and "Don't Hallucinate, Abstain" (Feng et al., ACL 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.00367). Multi-LLM collaboration/probing beats raw self-reported confidence.
- Practical: **don't use the LLM's self-reported confidence as the abstention signal**. Use evidence-structure rules (§7): number and tier of valid, quote-verified, independent items. Plus an independent verifier pass (second prompt or NLI model) that must agree.
- Report coverage vs. accuracy (a risk-coverage curve) in the test results. Juries like seeing the trade-off made explicit.

### 6.4 LLM-specific risk evidence
- **DeVerna et al., PNAS 2024** (https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2322823121): ChatGPT fact checks did **not** improve headline discernment. When wrong they **reduced belief in true headlines** mislabelled false, and when "unsure" they **increased belief in false headlines**. Human fact checks did improve discernment. Implications: (a) wrong confident verdicts are costly, so abstain rather than guess; (b) an "unsure" message must not read as soft confirmation. Always add „To nie znaczy, że informacja jest prawdziwa”.
- **Calibration gap** (Steyvers et al., Nature Machine Intelligence 2025, https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-024-00976-7): users overestimate LLM accuracy, and longer explanations raise trust regardless of correctness. So keep justifications short and evidence-anchored.

### 6.5 Presenting uncertainty to lay users
- **Kim et al., FAccT 2024** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00623): first-person uncertainty phrasing ("Nie mam pewności, ale…") reduced over-reliance and *increased* user accuracy. Impersonal phrasing had weaker, non-significant effects.
- **van der Bles et al., PNAS 2020** (https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1913678117): communicating uncertainty as a precise numeric range barely reduced trust. Vague verbal uncertainty reduced it more.
- **Warren, Shklovski & Augenstein, CHI 2025** "Show Me the Work" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09083): professional fact-checkers want explanations that **trace the reasoning path, reference specific evidence, and highlight uncertainty and information gaps**.
- **Amazeen et al. 2018** (JMCQ, https://doi.org/10.1177/1077699017710453): rating-scale graphics did not beat well-written contextual corrections. **The explanation matters more than the label.**
- **Design implication:** show a **categorical verdict + a qualitative strength level + an explicit premises checklist** (counts, tiers, dates), with first-person-style hedges in the BRAK/uncertain cases. **Avoid pseudo-precise percentages** derived from LLM confidence, which is not calibrated. Numeric facts that *are* well-founded can be shown: "2 niezależne źródła, najnowsze z 12.11.2026".

### 6.6 Warning labels, interstitials, backfire, prebunking
- **Labels work, including for people who distrust fact-checkers.** Martel & Rand, Current Opinion in Psychology 2023 (review): https://davidrand-cooperation.com/journal-articles/2024/7/21/misinformation-warning-labels-are-widely-effective-a-review-of-warning-effects-and-their-moderating-features. Martel & Rand, Nature Human Behaviour 2024: labels cut belief in false headlines by ≈28% and sharing by ≈25%, with smaller but significant effects among low-trust participants and **no backfire**. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-024-01973-x
- **Specific beats general.** Clayton et al., Political Behavior 2020 (https://doi.org/10.1007/s11109-019-09533-0): "Rated false" tags beat "Disputed". General "beware of fake news" warnings also reduce belief in **true** news, so don't spray generic warnings.
- **Implied truth effect.** Pennycook et al., Management Science 2020 (https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2019.3478): labelling some false items makes **unlabelled** false items seem more accurate. For us: a "brak dowodów" result must be clearly distinct from "potwierdzone", and we should say what *wasn't* checked.
- **Interstitials (click-through) vs. contextual labels.** Kaiser et al., USENIX Security 2021 (https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity21/presentation/kaiser): contextual warnings were largely ignored. **Interstitial warnings significantly changed behaviour** (users sought alternative sources), possibly through friction rather than understanding. Platform-reported figures point the same way: Facebook (Apr 2020) said 95% of users who saw a COVID warning screen did not click through, and Twitter (2020) reported fewer quote-tweets of labelled tweets. These are company-reported, not peer-reviewed. **For Prawdowid's browser extension:** a soft interstitial only on **content-level** matches to a verified fact-check (never on domain), always with „Pokaż mimo to”, never a hard block.
- **Backfire is rare.** Wood & Porter, Political Behavior 2019 ("The Elusive Backfire Effect", 52 issues, ~10k participants): https://doi.org/10.1007/s11109-018-9443-y. Swire-Thompson et al., JARMAC 2020: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jarmac.2020.06.006. The Debunking Handbook 2020 (Lewandowsky et al., https://www.climatechangecommunication.org/debunking-handbook-2020/) recommends **fact → warn about the myth (once) → explain the fallacy → fact again** (a "truth sandwich").
- **Accuracy prompts.** Pennycook et al., Nature 2021 (https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03344-2): nudging people to think about accuracy improves sharing quality. A cheap feature: „Zanim udostępnisz — czy to jest prawdziwe?” in bot replies.
- **Prebunking / inoculation.** Roozenbeek et al., Science Advances 2022 (https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abo6254): 90-second technique videos (emotional language, false dichotomies, scapegoating…) improved recognition on YouTube at scale. **Jigsaw's 2022 prebunking campaign in Poland, Czechia and Slovakia** (narratives identified with input from Demagog and NASK) showed its strongest discernment gain in **Poland**. These are self-reported results. https://medium.com/jigsaw/defanging-disinformations-threat-to-ukrainian-refugees-b164dbbc1c60
- **Design implications:** (1) specific, claim-level labels; (2) the explanation counts more than the badge; (3) never generic "uwaga, fake news" banners; (4) technique education (SPRAWDŹ tags) as a prebunking side-benefit; (5) the extension may use a soft interstitial for fact-check-matched content only.

## 7. Verdict decision rules (implementation spec)

### 7.1 Objects
- **Twierdzenie (claim):** an atomic, normalised, checkable proposition, with slots *who / what / when / where / quantity* and a **time scope**: `CURRENT` (present state), `DATED` (a past event or date), `TIMELESS`.
- **Dowód (evidence item):** `{doc_id, title, publisher, url, published_at, retrieved_at, fragment, stance ∈ {SUPPORTS, REFUTES, CONTEXT, IRRELEVANT}, tier ∈ {A,B,C,D}, independence_group, element ∈ {who,what,when,where,quantity,core}, context_type?}`

### 7.2 Source tiers (by source *type and process*, never by political orientation)
- **A, źródło pierwotne/urzędowe:** legal texts (ISAP/Dziennik Ustaw/Monitor Polski, EUR-Lex); official statistics (GUS/BDL, Eurostat, dane.gov.pl); official communications of the **competent** body (BIP, gov.pl, KE/Rada UE); court rulings; official registers; data of intergovernmental bodies (WHO, UN, OECD); the original recording or document of a quoted statement.
  - **Competence rule:** a public body is tier A only for facts within its competence, or for *the fact that it said or decided something*. A ministry press release is tier A for "the ministry announced X", not for contested evaluations.
- **B, zweryfikowane fact-checki:** articles by IFCN- or EFCSN-verified organisations, the EDMO/CEDMO repository, EUvsDisinfo cases, and the organisers' incident DB. These count only after a claim-match check.
- **C, wiarygodne źródła wtórne:** press agencies and news media meeting **published, symmetric** catalogue criteria: identifiable publisher and editor, a corrections policy, a registered press title or equivalent, original reporting. Also peer-reviewed research and recognised expert institutions.
- **D, pozostałe:** social posts, blogs, forums, Wikipedia, the analysed material itself. Can be shown as context or a pointer. **Never counted toward a threshold.**

### 7.3 Evidence validity gates (an item counts only if ALL pass)
1. **V1 Catalogue:** the document comes from the audited catalogue, or from an allow-listed primary domain fetched live, with both timestamps (published and retrieved) stored.
2. **V2 Verbatim quote:** the fragment is an exact substring of the stored document text (after whitespace/Unicode normalisation). Otherwise discard the item and log `QUOTE_UNVERIFIED`.
3. **V3 Stance agreement:** the stance is assigned by the main LLM **and** confirmed by an independent verifier (a second prompt with different framing or an NLI model) at confidence ≥ 0.7. If they disagree, the item becomes `CONTEXT`.
4. **V4 Relevance:** the fragment addresses a **core** element of the claim, not a peripheral detail.
5. **V5 Not model-generated, not injected:** text in a source that contains instructions aimed at the system goes to quarantine, the source is excluded from that check, and the event is logged as `INJECTION_ATTEMPT`.
6. **V6 Recency:** for `CURRENT` claims, the evidence must be the latest available from its series and inside the freshness window: fast-moving events 7 days; statistics the latest release; law the current consolidated text. Older items can only be `CONTEXT` and get the "materiał archiwalny" badge.

**Independence:** two items are independent if their publishers differ **and** neither derives from the other or from a common upstream for this fact. Detect "za PAP/Reuters", shared quotes, or text similarity > 0.85. `independence_group` = the upstream source if detected, otherwise the publisher. Count **groups**, not articles.

### 7.4 Ordered rules (first match wins; every result stores the IDs of the rules that fired)

**R0 Non-verdict statuses** (shown in a separate UI style, never with a verdict colour or icon):
- `BLAD_TECHNICZNY`: a retrieval or LLM error, timeout, missing API key, or rate limit. Text: „Nie udało się wykonać sprawdzenia z przyczyn technicznych. To nie jest ocena treści.”
- `POTRZEBNE_DOPRECYZOWANIE`: no extractable claim, an ambiguous referent, or multiple incompatible readings. Ask **one** clarifying question.
- `POZA_ZAKRESEM`: e.g. a request for personal medical or legal advice, or a question about a private individual.

**R1 NIE PODLEGA WERYFIKACJI:** the claim type is opinion or value judgment, normative ("powinno"), prediction about the future, hypothetical, satire clearly marked as such, an unfalsifiable statement, or someone's private mental state. If the statement embeds a factual premise ("Rząd, który podniósł VAT do 30%, jest zły"), extract the premise as a separate claim and evaluate it.

**R2 Conflict guard → BRAK WYSTARCZAJĄCYCH DOWODÓW (flag `ZRODLA_SPRZECZNE`)** fires when there is at least one valid SUPPORT and at least one valid REFUTE item, each of tier A or B, or ≥2 independent tier-C groups on each side. Two exceptions:
- (a) the same publisher issued a newer revision of the same data: use the newer one and note the revision;
- (b) the disagreement comes from different definitions or periods: treat it as context and continue to R3–R5 with a caveat.

Always show both sides side by side (scenario C).

**R3 FAŁSZYWE:** the core element is refuted by **≥1 valid tier-A REFUTE**, **or** by **≥2 independent valid REFUTE groups from tiers B/C with ≥1 of them tier B**. In addition, there is **no** valid tier A/B SUPPORT for the core element.

**R4 WPROWADZAJĄCE W BŁĄD:** the core factual element **is supported** (it meets the R5 thresholds for that element) **and** ≥1 valid tier A–C `CONTEXT` item shows at least one of:
- (a) `NIEAKTUALNE`: the material or data is older than presented, or has been superseded;
- (b) `FALSZYWY_KONTEKST`: a different place or event (e.g. the earliest appearance of an image predates the claimed event);
- (c) `WYBIORCZOSC`: an omitted denominator or trend, or a cherry-picked period that changes the conclusion;
- (d) `BLEDNY_WNIOSEK`: an unsupported causal or general claim drawn from true facts;
- (e) `ZNIEKSZTALCONY_CYTAT`: a truncated quote that changes the meaning;
- (f) `MIESZANE`: a false element that changes the main message.

The sub-type must be named and sourced.

**R5 POTWIERDZONE:** every core slot is covered by a valid SUPPORT item, there is no valid tier A/B REFUTE, and V6 recency holds. In addition, one of these holds:
- **(i)** ≥1 tier A **plus** ≥1 independent confirmation from tiers A–C; **or**
- **(ii)** ≥2 independent groups from tiers B/C with ≥1 of them tier B; **or**
- **(iii)** a **single self-authoritative tier A** source, where the claim is about what that body itself published or decided, e.g. "Ustawa X weszła w życie 1.01.2026" with the Dz.U. entry as evidence (scenario A).

**R6 Default → BRAK WYSTARCZAJĄCYCH DOWODÓW**, with reason codes: `BRAK_MATERIALOW`, `JEDNO_ZRODLO`, `TYLKO_SLABE_ZRODLA` (tier D only), `NIEAKTUALNE_DOWODY`, `CZESCIOWE_POKRYCIE` (some slots unverified, which are listed), `CYTAT_NIEZWERYFIKOWANY`, `ZBYT_SWIEZE` (event < 24 h old). Always state **what could not be established** and **what would settle it** („Rozstrzygnąłby to np. komunikat [instytucji] lub dane [GUS]”).

### 7.5 Strength of the basis ("siła podstawy"), shown as text plus an icon, never as a percentage
- **MOCNA:** the verdict holds with ≥1 tier A **and** ≥2 independent groups in total, everything within the recency window, and no conflicting tier A–C item (even as context).
- **UMIARKOWANA:** the verdict thresholds are met, but not MOCNA (e.g. tier B/C only, or the R5(iii) single self-authoritative source).
- For BRAK the strength is reported as „Podstawa: niewystarczająca”, with the reason codes.
- The checklist is always shown: ✔/✖ źródło pierwotne · liczba niezależnych potwierdzeń: N · najnowszy dowód: data · zgodność fragmentu: tak/częściowa · sprzeczne źródła: tak/nie.

### 7.6 Stricter rules for high-risk topics
High-risk topics: security/military, elections and electoral procedure, public health or danger to life, crisis/evacuation, sanctions/FIMI-relevant topics.
- FAŁSZYWE and POTWIERDZONE require **≥1 tier A, or ≥1 tier B + ≥1 independent tier C**. Otherwise the result is BRAK.
- **Escalation:** any BRAK on a high-risk topic, **or** a normalised-claim cluster with ≥3 BRAK results in 24 h, creates or updates **one** moderator case (deduplicated by cluster ID).
- The UI distinguishes **„Zarekomendowaliśmy przekazanie sprawy analitykowi”** (recommendation created) from **„Sprawa została przekazana (nr #123)”**. The second appears only after the queue confirms receipt (challenge requirement).

### 7.7 Source-status notices (a separate box, never inputs to R1–R6)
- An EU-sanctioned entity (e.g. under Council Reg. (EU) 2022/350 and later) gets a neutral note: „Ten podmiot jest objęty sankcjami UE [podstawa, data]. Informacja o statusie źródła nie przesądza o prawdziwości tej treści.”
- Domain or channel present in the incident catalogue: „Ten kanał pojawia się w katalogu incydentów [nazwa, data]. Ocena powyżej opiera się wyłącznie na dowodach dotyczących tej treści.” Show it only in explicitly configured cases.
- Impersonation (typosquatting of a known outlet or institution): „Adres przypomina [x], ale nim nie jest.”
- SPRAWDŹ rhetoric signals (S/P/D) are educational only (§1.4).

### 7.8 Determinism & channel consistency
Cache the result by `(normalised_claim_hash, catalogue_version, rules_version)`. The web UI and the bot read the same record, so the same input gets the same substantive result (scenario G). Use temperature 0 for classification steps. Store `rules_version` in the audit log.

### 7.9 Config sketch
```yaml

validity: {stance_min_conf: 0.7, require_verifier_agreement: true, quote_exact: true, dup_similarity: 0.85}

  false:      {any_of: [{tierA_refute: 1}, {indep_refute_groups_BC: 2, min_tierB: 1}], forbid: {tierAB_support: 1}}
```

## 8. What an MSZ/NASK jury will value

### 8.1 Features and wording that score
1. **"Nie jesteśmy sędzią prawdy"** framing, in the header and on the Metodologia page: „Pokazujemy, co mówią źródła — ocenę zostawiamy Tobie.” This echoes the challenge's own closing sentence.
2. **Evidence cards** that separate „Co mówi źródło” (verbatim fragment, title, publisher, date published / retrieved, link) from „Nasza interpretacja” (model text), with a different visual style for each.
3. **"Dlaczego taki wynik?"** checklist per claim (§7.5) with the rule IDs available in a "szczegóły techniczne" drawer, so the strength of the verdict is visibly derived from premises.
4. **First-class abstention:** BRAK with reason codes, „czego nie udało się ustalić”, „co mogłoby to rozstrzygnąć”, and „To nie znaczy, że informacja jest prawdziwa ani fałszywa.” Technical failure and clarification requests in a different, non-verdict style.
5. **Claim decomposition shown to the user**: „W Twojej wiadomości znaleźliśmy 3 twierdzenia: 2 faktyczne, 1 opinię.”
6. **Archive / date warning:** „Ten materiał pochodzi z 2019 r.” (scenario B), plus publication and retrieval timestamps everywhere.
7. **Public pages:** Metodologia (§9), **Katalog źródeł** (inclusion/exclusion/update criteria, versioned, downloadable as CSV), **Rejestr korekt**, **Zgłoś błąd** form, AI and data-processing notice. This maps 1:1 onto IFCN 2/4/5 and EFCSN Art. 2–4.
8. **Human in the loop:** moderator queue with clustering/deduplication, honest "recommended" vs "forwarded" status, moderator override recorded in the audit log, and a published „tryb wspierania decyzji” for the pilot (challenge §9).
9. **SPRAWDŹ (NASK) educational panel** + technique tags (fałszywy kontekst, cherry picking, podszywanie się…) linked to NASK materials. Prebunking is something MSZ's own Council recommends.
10. **Signposting to official channels**: zglos-dezinformacje.nask.pl, SMS 8080 (CERT Polska), platform DSA report. Never pretend Prawdowid has reported anything itself.
11. **Analyst view:** repeated unanswered questions, clusters, ABCDE B/D/E indicators, STIX 2.1 / OpenCTI export with DISARM tags marked as *suggestions*, TLP markings.
12. **AI transparency (AI Act Art. 50, applicable from 2.08.2026; check Digital Omnibus changes):** „Rozmawiasz z automatycznym systemem wykorzystującym SI.” Name the model, provider and processing location (prefer EEA or self-hosted), and give a cost estimate.
13. **Privacy (RODO):** minimisation, automatic masking of PESEL/phone/e-mail/addresses, a stated retention period (e.g. 30 days), „Usuń moją sesję”, no training on user data.
14. **Accessibility:** WCAG 2.1 AA (ustawa o dostępności cyfrowej applies to public bodies, and MSZ would deploy it). Verdicts never conveyed by colour alone. Plain language (B1 level, short sentences, no jargon). Tested with a screen reader.
15. **Symmetry / bias test** in the eval report: mirrored claims about different parties, institutions and outlets give identical rule paths. Report the verdict distribution by topic.
16. **Prompt-injection log** shown in the demo (scenario E): „Wykryto i zignorowano polecenie w treści źródła.”

### 8.2 Wording guide (citizen-facing)
| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| „nieprawdziwe”, „źródła temu przeczą” | „kłamstwo” (implies intent), „fake news”, „propaganda” |
| „wprowadzające w błąd — brakuje kontekstu: …” | „manipulacja Kremla”, any actor attribution |
| „Nie znaleźliśmy wystarczających dowodów” | „Prawdopodobnie prawda/fałsz”, „Nie wiadomo” (without reasons) |
| „Według [GUS, 12.11.2026] …” | „Na pewno”, „100%”, „Udowodniono” |
| „Nie mam pewności, bo …” (first person, short) | Long, confident-sounding explanations |
| „To opinia — nie da się jej sprawdzić faktami” | „Twoja informacja jest bez sensu” |
| „Ten podmiot jest objęty sankcjami UE — to nie przesądza o prawdziwości treści” | „Niewiarygodne źródło”, „portal propagandowy” |

### 8.3 Pitfalls that would cost points (or fail the boundary conditions)
- **Domain/outlet-level verdicts.** Explicitly forbidden: „Nie wolno przesądzać o prawdziwości… wyłącznie na podstawie domeny, autora, kanału, popularności, liczby udostępnień”. Flagging *tvrepublika.pl* or any other outlet (wPolityce, OKO.press, Gazeta Wyborcza, TVN24, RT…) as a whole violates this. Every outlet's content is treated as *analysed material*. If an outlet is in the evidence catalogue, the inclusion criteria must be published and applied symmetrically.
- **Partisan skew in the catalogue or test set** (e.g. tier-C media only from one side of the political spectrum, or test claims only about one party). Use process-based inclusion criteria and publish the list.
- **"Nieweryfikowalne" → "nie podlega weryfikacji" mislabelling.** Demagog's *nieweryfikowalne* means "no evidence available", which is BRAK in our scheme.
- **Fabricated or hallucinated sources** are a boundary-condition failure (zero points for premium features). Hence the V1/V2 gates and showing the stored copy.
- **Presenting technical failure, a missing key or a rate limit as a verdict** (scenario J).
- **Claiming forwarding to a human when only a recommendation exists** (scenario H).
- **Hard-coding test examples** or seeding answers in the prompt (explicitly checked by the jury).
- **Claiming full reverse image search** without a working API or a clearly bounded local index.
- **Linking to the disinformation itself with dofollow links** (NASK Kodeks Dobrych Praktyk). Use screenshots, archived snippets or `rel="nofollow ugc"`.
- **Linking `sprawdzam.info`**, which is now an unrelated site.
- **Calling the user's item "dezinformacja" or attributing it to a state actor.** Intent and attribution are analyst judgements (EEAS IMS/attribution logic), not citizen-facing outputs.
- **Generic "beware of fake news" banners on every answer.** These reduce trust in true news (Clayton et al. 2020).
- **Numeric "pewność 87%" badges** from uncalibrated LLM self-reports.
- **Non-EEA processing without disclosure**, or personal data and keys in the repo.

## 9. Ready-to-use page text: „Metodologia” (Polish, ~445 words)

> **Metodologia Prawdowida**
>
> **Prawdowid nie jest sędzią prawdy.** Pomagamy sprawdzić doniesienie i pokazujemy, na jakich źródłach opieramy odpowiedź. Ostateczną ocenę zostawiamy Tobie — zachęcamy, by zajrzeć do źródeł.
>
> **Jak sprawdzamy**
> 1. **Wyodrębniamy twierdzenia.** Dzielimy wiadomość na pojedyncze twierdzenia i oddzielamy fakty od opinii, ocen i przewidywań.
> 2. **Szukamy dowodów** wyłącznie w jawnym katalogu źródeł: dokumenty urzędowe i dane publiczne (np. ISAP, EUR-Lex, GUS, dane.gov.pl), artykuły organizacji fact-checkingowych stosujących standardy IFCN lub EFCSN oraz wiarygodne źródła wtórne.
> 3. **Porównujemy** każde twierdzenie z konkretnym fragmentem źródła. Sprawdzamy datę, miejsce i kontekst oraz to, czy źródła są od siebie niezależne.
> 4. **Podajemy wynik** według stałych, jawnych reguł — nie według „wrażenia” modelu.
> 5. **Zapisujemy przebieg** sprawdzenia: pytanie, czas, źródła i wynik.
>
> **Możliwe wyniki**
> - **Potwierdzone** — wiarygodne źródła potwierdzają wszystkie istotne elementy twierdzenia.
> - **Fałszywe** — wiarygodne źródła przeczą twierdzeniu.
> - **Wprowadzające w błąd** — część faktów się zgadza, ale brakuje kontekstu, dane są nieaktualne albo prowadzą do błędnego wniosku.
> - **Brak wystarczających dowodów** — nie znaleźliśmy dość materiałów albo źródła są sprzeczne. To nie znaczy, że informacja jest prawdziwa, ani że jest fałszywa.
> - **Nie podlega weryfikacji** — to opinia, ocena lub przewidywanie, których nie da się sprawdzić faktami.
>
> Awarię techniczną i potrzebę doprecyzowania pytania komunikujemy osobno — to nie są werdykty.
>
> **Siła podstawy**
> Przy każdym wyniku pokazujemy, z czego wynika: rodzaj i datę źródeł, zgodność fragmentu z twierdzeniem oraz liczbę niezależnych potwierdzeń. Źródło pierwotne waży więcej niż jego omówienie. Kilka artykułów powtarzających tę samą depeszę liczymy jako jedno źródło. Każde źródło opisujemy tytułem, wydawcą, datą, linkiem i cytowanym fragmentem.
>
> **Czego nie robimy**
> - Nie oceniamy prawdziwości treści na podstawie samej domeny, autora, kanału, popularności ani liczby udostępnień. Nie oznaczamy żadnego medium jako „fałszywego” w całości.
> - Jeśli podmiot jest np. objęty sankcjami UE, pokazujemy to jako osobną, neutralną informację — nie jako dowód.
> - Nie przedstawiamy odpowiedzi modelu językowego jako faktu bez źródła.
> - Nie oceniamy intencji autorów ani poglądów politycznych. Te same reguły stosujemy wobec wszystkich.
>
> **Sztuczna inteligencja**
> Odpowiedź przygotowuje automatyczny system z użyciem modelu językowego. Model pomaga zrozumieć pytanie, wyszukać i porównać materiały oraz sformułować odpowiedź. Polecenia ukryte w analizowanych treściach ignorujemy. Sprawy nierozstrzygnięte i wysokiego ryzyka mogą trafić do moderatora — zawsze informujemy, czy sprawę faktycznie przekazano.
>
> **Błędy i korekty**
> Każdy może zgłosić błąd przyciskiem „Zgłoś błąd”. Poprawione wyniki oznaczamy datą i opisem zmiany w publicznym rejestrze korekt. Wynik odzwierciedla stan wiedzy z dnia sprawdzenia.
>
> **Prywatność**
> Przetwarzamy tylko to, co potrzebne do sprawdzenia. Automatycznie maskujemy dane osobowe. Historię sesji możesz usunąć. Dane przechowujemy przez [X] dni, przetwarzamy je w [lokalizacja].
>
> **Sprawdzaj także sam**
> Skorzystaj z metody SPRAWDŹ przygotowanej przez NASK (nask.pl/dezinfo). Dezinformację zgłosisz na zglos-dezinformacje.nask.pl, a podejrzane SMS-y przekażesz na numer 8080 (CERT Polska).
